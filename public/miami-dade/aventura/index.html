Aventura Bees in Condo Towers, Garages and Docks

Aventura is mostly vertical, so a colony here is usually a building problem before it is a yard problem. You need someone who can get past the gatehouse, satisfy the board and reach the spot.

Quick answer

High rises set around the Turnberry course make up most of Aventura, so the opening moves of a removal here are paperwork and access rather than tools: a COI sent to management, a service elevator or roof key booked, and a ruling on whether the comb sits in a unit or a common element. Only then does the crew go up, and every bee leaves the building alive, bound for a beekeeper's yard. A planter or valve box reachable from the pavement tends to fall near $300 to $400; comb thirty floors up or sealed behind stucco and drywall can climb into the thousands.

If anyone is being stung

Stings happening now? Move inside, into a lobby, stairwell or unit, and let the door close behind you. Anyone wheezing, with a swollen tongue or lips, or stung many times needs 911 first. Ask the front desk to keep residents off that balcony, deck or garage level. Once everyone is behind a closed door, call us, even at 3 a.m.; a neighbor taking stings moves to the front of our list.

Before the city existed, this stretch went by the name Turnberry, and building started in the early 1970s. Aventura did not become a city until November 1995. Today it is largely residential high rises set around a golf course, with the Intracoastal on one side and Biscayne Boulevard on the other.

That shape decides how bees show up. A scout bee hunting for a cavity does not care whether a gap sits on the ground floor or the thirtieth. Rooftop mechanical housings, garage expansion joints, planter boxes on pool decks and the hollow bases of light poles all read as shelter. What changes in Aventura is the response: the colony usually sits on shared property, several people have to sign off, and the work has to fit a building's rules about contractors, hours and elevators.

Where colonies tuck into Aventura buildings

Rooftop mechanical areas

Equipment enclosures, chiller housings and the voids beside them stay dry and sheltered on a tower roof. Bees flying in and out up there can go unnoticed for weeks, until a maintenance tech opens a panel and finds comb.

Garage expansion joints

Parking structures under and beside condo towers have joints, conduit chases and cracked block that open into dark cavities. A steady line of bees along one wall of a garage level is worth a call before residents start parking under it.

Pool deck planters and palms

Raised planters, hollow palm boots and the irrigation boxes feeding them sit beside the one place everyone walks barefoot. Bees also drink from pool edges, so foragers on the deck do not always mean a hive is close.

Docks and seawall gaps

Along the Intracoastal and the bay, dock boxes, utility pedestals and gaps in seawall caps give a colony a dry pocket right where owners load boats. Those spots are tight and slow to open safely.

Golf-course-side landscaping

The course at the center of the city sits among lakes and landscaped grounds, where irrigation valve and meter boxes are buried in the beds. A colony low in one of those boxes is usually simpler and less costly to reach than one high on a tower.

Notes on Aventura communities

  • Williams Island

    Williams Island is a section of Aventura, and a visit there starts at your building's front desk and whatever entry check your association runs. Give us the building name and who holds the contractor list, and the visit can be cleared before anyone drives over.

  • Point East

    Point East buildings go by letter, and the city's Founders Day shuttle schedule lists them that way, from A through T. When you call, the letter matters as much as the street address: it tells us which manager receives our COI and which entrance the crew should use.

  • Mystic Pointe

    Mystic Pointe towers carry numbers, 100 through 600. Bees reported on a balcony in one tower and a planter in another are two separate colonies until proven otherwise, so note the tower and floor for each sighting.

  • Turnberry Isle and the Yacht Club

    Turnberry Isle North and South, the Turnberry Gatehouse and the Yacht Club each appear by name on the city's Founders Day shuttle schedule. If your building has a gatehouse, expect a check-in there, and if the colony is near a slip, we will ask which dock and whether a boat needs moving.

  • Country Club Drive and the golf ring

    Country Club Drive circles the Turnberry course, and its paved promenade looks out over lakes and residential high rises. Swarms resting in course-side trees can move on alone; bees entering a wall or box will not.

  • Biscayne Lakes Gardens

    Biscayne Lakes Gardens is another name on that shuttle schedule. If your building there has a management office, start with them so the insurance paperwork and access plan are settled before the visit.

Who calls, and who signs off

In a single-family house, the owner decides. In an Aventura condo, the decision often runs through a property manager, a board and sometimes a building engineer, and the bees do not wait for the next meeting. It helps to know where the colony sits. Bees inside your own enclosed balcony planter may be a unit issue; bees behind the exterior stucco or under the roof membrane are usually a common element that the association handles. Your declaration is the final word on that, not us.

What we can do is make the approval easy. Management can have our COI on request, the scope is spelled out before anything is opened, and photos plus an itemized invoice can follow for the board file. If the colony has to come out of a wall or roof, the patch is done by our own crew of licensed contractors, roofers and painters, which spares the association from hiring and scheduling a second trade. Our high-rise removal page and association and property manager page go deeper on how that paperwork moves.

Water at the edges and in the middle

The Intracoastal and Dumfoundling Bay run along Aventura's east side, the southern boundary lies in Maule Lake, and lakes line the golf course at the center. Honey bees need water every day, especially in summer heat, so pools, fountains, irrigation drips and boat decks all draw foragers.

That creates a common mix-up. Dozens of bees sipping at the edge of a pool are usually just drinking and will fly off to a hive that may be blocks away. A cluster of bees hanging in a ball on a railing is a swarm in transit, which you can read more about on our swarm pickup page. Bees streaming steadily into one slot in a wall, a dock box or a seawall cap are the ones that have moved in. For waterfront spots, see docks, seawalls and boats.

When the unit sits empty

Some Aventura units stay closed for long stretches while owners are away. A quiet balcony with planters, vents and gaps nobody checks is just the kind of undisturbed spot scouts look for, and nobody is there to notice them. Owners who leave for a season can ask the front desk or engineer to glance at balconies and roof hatches now and then; our seasonal-home guide lists the signs worth reporting. When a colony is hidden behind finished surfaces, thermal imaging helps pinpoint it so only the right section is opened.

How an Aventura visit runs

  1. Clear the front desk

    We confirm the building, unit or common area, and what the gatehouse or lobby needs from a contractor. Your manager has our COI in hand before the date is set.

  2. Book the access

    Service elevator, roof hatch key, garage level or dock: whatever the colony needs is reserved in advance, so residents are not held up and the crew is not waiting downstairs.

  3. Remove the colony alive

    The whole colony, wax included, is lifted out intact and driven to a beekeeper who keeps it. Nearby residents are asked to keep balcony doors shut while the area is open, which keeps everyone calm.

  4. Seal, repair, report

    The entry is closed, any opened surface is repaired by our own crews, and photos with an itemized invoice go to whoever needs them for the board.

Questions people ask us

Our management office wants insurance paperwork before you arrive. Is that a problem?

Not at all. A COI can be sent to the association or management company before the visit. Ask your manager where it should go and pass that along when you call, so it is in hand by the time the date is booked.

I live on a high floor. Can bees really be up here?

Yes. Honey bees forage and scout well above ground level, and a tower offers plenty of sheltered gaps around balconies, planters and rooftop equipment. A handful of bees on a railing may just be passing through. Bees going in and out of one opening for several days point to a colony that needs attention.

Who pays: me or the association?

Your condominium documents settle it, along with the exact location of the comb. Bees inside something you own and maintain may be your cost; bees in the building envelope are usually a common element. Check with your manager first. We can quote either party, and the quote is free.

What will this cost for a planter or a valve box at ground level?

Ground-level jobs like a planter, an irrigation box or a low wall typically land around $300 to $400. Colonies high on a building, under roofing, or inside a wall that must be opened and patched can run into the thousands. Calling us out after dark or over a weekend is priced higher than booking a regular weekday slot. See our cost page for what drives the number.

If you seal the spot and bees come back, then what?

Our sealing work carries a warranty: a fresh colony in that same closed-up gap gets another visit from us. Leftover wax and scent are what pull new scouts to an old cavity, as this field guide article describes, so the space is emptied and cleaned rather than simply plugged.
