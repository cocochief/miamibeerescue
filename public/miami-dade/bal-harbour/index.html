Bee removal in Bal Harbour, from the jetty to 96th Street

Bal Harbour fits ocean towers, a gated residential community and an open-air shopping center onto well under a square mile of land. Bees find the gaps in all three, and each has its own rules for letting a crew in.

Quick answer

Because Bal Harbour buried its utilities, meters and valves sit in hollow boxes at sidewalk level, so a colony may be under a lid in the lawn. On the oceanfront towers, a balcony planter or wall panel is usually a common element, so the association signs off first. In both places nothing is sprayed: the bees leave alive for a beekeeper's yard, and we close the opening behind them. A meter box you can reach from the sidewalk usually lands near $300 to $400, while a tower facade that has to be opened and patched can climb into the thousands. The quote itself costs nothing.

If anyone is being stung

Bees chasing people? Get away quickly, cover your face and head for the nearest lobby, garage or vehicle, then put a door between you and them. Anyone wheezing, with lips or tongue puffing up, or stung dozens of times needs 911 now. After that, ring us at any hour; a stung person always jumps the queue. Tower residents: slide the balcony door shut and tell the concierge.

Around Bal Harbour

  • Incorporated1946, as a village
  • Footprint0.64 square miles in all, more than a third of it water
  • Population3,093 at the 2020 census
  • LayoutPlanned community designed by Harland Bartholomew & Associates
  • Bal Harbour ShopsOpen-air center that opened in 1965 on 16 acres

Bal Harbour sits at the northern tip of the barrier island, with the Atlantic to the east, the bay to the west and Baker's Haulover Inlet closing off the top. It was laid out as a planned community, incorporated in 1946, and became the first planned community in Florida to put its utilities underground. That last detail matters to bees more than most people would guess. Without overhead lines, the meters, valves and pull boxes sit at ground level in hollow boxes and vaults, and a scout hunting for a dark, dry cavity treats a cracked lid as a vacancy sign.

The rest of the village offers bees two very different kinds of shelter. Along Collins Avenue, oceanfront condominiums and hotels give them balcony planters, rooftop equipment and hollow cladding far above the sidewalk. In the gated residential community, homes, garages and garden walls sit behind controlled entrances. Each side has its own gatekeepers, a board or a gate, and a removal goes far more smoothly when those people are lined up before the truck pulls in.

Where colonies settle in Bal Harbour

Buried utility boxes

Because the village put its utilities underground, water meters, irrigation valves and electrical pull boxes sit in ground-level boxes beside lawns and sidewalks. A cracked or badly seated lid leaves a dark chamber with steady temperatures, close to perfect for a new colony. Do not lift the lid to look; bees guard that doorway fiercely.

Balcony planters on the towers

On the oceanfront condominiums, bees use hollow spaces behind built-in planters, gaps around railing posts and voids in decorative panels. The entrance may be a thin slot you only spot by watching the traffic.

Rooftops and mechanical rooms

Cooling equipment, louvered screens and roof hatches on the taller buildings keep rain off a colony. These places sit behind locked doors, so the building engineer has to open them, and any work at height is planned with the manager ahead of time.

Garages and walls in the gated section

Inside the gated community, a colony can move into a soffit, a garage ceiling, a hollow block wall or a gap behind wooden trim. A thermal camera shows where the warm cluster sits behind the finish, so only that patch has to be opened.

Shop plantings and overhangs

Bal Harbour Shops is an open-air center, so its planters, overhangs and sign cavities are outdoors where a passing swarm can land. Bees near a busy walkway are a crowd-safety matter first, and the crew's timing is set with center management.

Beach paths and the jetty

Near the jetty at the north end and along the beach access paths, shore plantings can hold a resting swarm. A swarm hanging on a branch is usually resting while its scouts look for a home, and that cluster tends to move off after a day or two, but call if it stays or people use that path.

Notes for each part of the village

  • The Collins Avenue oceanfront

    The ocean side of Collins Avenue is lined with condominium and hotel towers, among them the St. Regis Bal Harbour and Oceana Bal Harbour. Expect a loading dock, a sign-in at the front desk and a manager who expects proof of insurance on file before anyone steps onto a balcony.

  • The gated residential community

    Homes here sit behind controlled entrances such as the Harbour Way and Bal Bay Drive gates. Give the gate our company name ahead of time so the truck is not held at the arm.

  • Bal Harbour Shops and 96th Street

    The shops opened in 1965 on 16 acres, and Village Hall is on 96th Street nearby. Foot traffic runs all day here, so any work beside a walkway is booked with management and coned off before the colony is touched.

  • The north end by the inlet

    Baker's Haulover Inlet bounds the village at the top, with the county's Haulover Park across the water, and the jetty beach access sits here. Wind off the water makes a hanging swarm harder to box, so a pickup is timed for calmer hours when that is possible.

  • The bay side and Indian Creek

    On the west edge, the village faces Biscayne Bay, with Indian Creek between it and Bay Harbor Islands. If bees keep circling near the water, check dock boxes, boat covers and gaps along the seawall.

Condo boards, insurance papers and the service elevator

In an oceanfront tower, the person who calls is rarely the person who can say yes. A unit owner spots bees at a balcony planter, but the planter, the railing and the wall behind it are usually common elements, so the association or its manager has to approve the work. Plan for that from the first message. Send us the manager's contact along with your photo, and our COI can be emailed straight to the office so nobody waits on paperwork.

The crew then works the way the building wants: check-in at the desk, the service entrance, the elevator the staff assigns, and protection for hallway floors. If the colony is inside a wall panel or a column cover, our high-rise removal work and the property manager process cover the steps. Licensed and insured crews make the board's file easy to close, and if the office asks, we add pictures of the opened cavity and a line-by-line bill.

Getting through the gate in the residential section

The gated section was built to keep unexpected vehicles out, and it does that well. Before a visit, tell the gate who is coming and when, or add the company to your guest list through the usual channel. If you are not home, say where the bees are and whether a side yard, garage or pool area can be reached without entering the house.

Inside the gate, the likely spots are the same ones found on any well-kept home: a soffit, a garage ceiling, a hollow wall, a hedge or a palm. Our pages on bees inside walls, soffit and eave hives and palm and tree colonies explain how each one comes apart and goes back together. If scouts are already testing a gap but no comb has been built, the scout bee guide tells you what to watch for in the next day or two.

Bees under a lid by the sidewalk

A steady line of bees dropping into the ground beside a sidewalk or a lawn edge usually means a meter or valve box, not a nest in the soil. In a village with its utilities buried, those boxes are everywhere, and some carry live electrical or water connections. Keep people and pets back, do not pour anything into the box, and do not pry the lid.

The crew lifts the cover with the colony contained, cuts out the comb, draws the bees by soft suction into a screened carrier and wipes the chamber clean of wax so nothing lingers to tempt a fresh swarm. If the box belongs to a utility, its owner may need to know before the lid comes off. The meter and valve box page has more on how these jobs run, while the return-visit guide covers what leftover comb does to a site.

Repairs that fit a carefully kept village

Once the colony is out, there is usually an opening to close: a cut in stucco, a lifted roof tile, a panel off a soffit. Our own licensed contractors, roofers and painters do that work, so one company opens the hole, closes it and stands behind it. Our workmanship carries a warranty: should a colony try that same sealed gap again, a crew returns.

The village runs its own Building Department and an architectural board, so before any change visible from the street, ask Village Hall whether the patch needs a permit or sign-off. A like-for-like repair is the usual goal. The repairs after removal page lists what can be rebuilt, and the cost page explains why a job with repairs runs well past a simple ground-level removal.

When you are away for the season

When a unit or home sits empty for months, a colony can settle and grow with nobody there to see it. A neighbor or the front desk may be the first to notice bees at a window or a balcony. If that happens, you can arrange the whole job remotely: photos, a free quote, a COI to the manager, and pictures plus an itemized invoice afterward if you ask.

Before you fly out, skim our seasonal-owner checklist and leave a key holder's number with the desk. After a storm, check the storm damage guide too, since torn cladding or a shifted tile can give a swarm a new door. If you are selling, the home sale guide covers bees found during an inspection.

How a visit runs in Bal Harbour

  1. Picture, place, paperwork

    A snapshot of the bees plus the building or street name starts it. When a condo office, a residential association or shop management is involved, our COI reaches them first, so approval is settled before anyone drives over.

  2. Gate or front desk

    In the residential section the crew is added to the guest list at the gate. At a tower, the crew signs in at the desk and uses the service entrance and elevator the building assigns.

  3. Find it and take it out alive

    Thermal imaging pins down the warm cluster. Only a small opening is made; bees are drawn out by low suction, each sheet of comb is cut free, and a beekeeper takes the colony to its new yard.

  4. Seal, repair, paperwork

    The entry is sealed, and our own contractors handle roofing, stucco or paint. Boards that keep files can ask for before-and-after pictures and an itemized bill.

Questions people ask us

Can you work inside the gated residential section if I am not home?

Often, yes, as long as the gate knows the crew is coming and the bees can be reached from outside the house, such as a soffit, a garage exterior or a yard. Tell us where the entrance is, and ask for photos of the finished work. Anything that needs access to the interior has to wait for you or someone you authorize.

My condo association wants proof of insurance. Can you send it to the manager?

Yes. Give us the manager's name and office contact, and our COI goes to that office directly. That usually clears the job faster than routing paperwork through the unit owner. If the board keeps records, ask and we will send pictures of the job along with a line-by-line bill.

Bees are going into the ground by the sidewalk. What is that?

In Bal Harbour it is most likely a buried meter, valve or pull box, since the village has its utilities underground. Keep people back and leave the lid alone; some boxes hold live connections. The colony and its comb can be taken out live, and the chamber cleaned so it does not attract the next swarm.

Will the bees be killed or kept alive?

Alive, every time. Extermination is never on the table; the whole colony travels to a beekeeper who rehomes it. Once comb is built, bees rarely abandon it on their own, so a live removal is how they get moved. Killing a colony inside a wall also leaves comb and honey to rot, which draws pests and stains finishes, so live removal is the cleaner result as well.

Our unit sits empty most of the summer. Can the job be handled while we are away?

Yes. With your permission and the building's, the job can be arranged by phone and text: photos, a free quote, the COI to the manager and a crew scheduled through the front desk. Ask, and the photos and itemized invoice are emailed to you after the visit.

Do I need village approval for the repair afterward?

Village Hall on 96th Street is the place to ask, since its Building Department and architectural board set the rules for exterior work. Most bee repairs are like-for-like patches, and our own contractors match the existing finish. Ask about permits before the patch goes on.
