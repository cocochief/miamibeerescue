Quick answer

Bee damage repair is the work that follows a removal: the square of stucco cut away, the lifted roof tiles, the drywall pulled from a ceiling, the soffit panel taken down. Across Miami-Dade, the people doing it are our own licensed tradespeople, a roofer for tile and a painter for the finish, so nobody has to find a second company once the bees are gone. Once the bees are relocated, comb and honey come out, the old entrance is sealed so the scent cannot pull in a new swarm, and then the surface is rebuilt and painted to match.

If anyone is being stung

If bees are pouring out of a fresh crack or a half-finished patch and someone is being stung, get everyone indoors, close the windows and call. People being stung come first. The line stays staffed around the clock, even mid-repair, and our 24-hour response guarantee covers a patch that bees have reopened. Do not tape or foam the opening while bees are still flying from it.

What the house looks like once the bees are gone

  • A rectangle cut through the stucco face of a block wall, with open block cells or bare lath showing where the comb hung.
  • Barrel or flat roof tiles stacked beside the opening and underlayment exposed where the colony sat between tile and deck.
  • A missing square of drywall in a ceiling or closet wall, sometimes with an amber honey stain spreading through the paint around it.
  • A soffit panel or vented strip taken down along the eave, leaving the gap the bees used open to rain and to scouting bees.
  • Sun-faded paint around the opening that no longer matches the color code on the can left in the garage.

What is going on behind it

A colony cannot be pulled out through its entrance hole. Every sheet of comb has to come out with it, and comb is often attached across several feet of cavity, so the surface over it has to be opened. Once the bees leave, nothing fans or guards that wax. Honey can seep into drywall and block, wax moth larvae move in to eat the comb, and the smell of an old nest draws swarm scouts looking for a home. That is why the repair is not cosmetic. The cavity has to be stripped clean, the entry closed, and only then is the outer skin rebuilt.

Every surface gets its own trade

Stucco is rebuilt the way it was first applied: over lath that means a scratch coat, a brown coat and a finish coat, while over block the layers are fewer and thinner. The last coat is troweled smooth, floated to a sand finish or textured by hand to match the wall around it. Drywall is cut back to the nearest framing, replaced, taped, mudded and primed. Roof tile is a roofer's job, reset over new underlayment so water sheds the way it did before. Soffits get new panels and, where vents are involved, screen behind them. A painter finishes every one of these, which is the step most patch jobs skip. For the removal side, see hives inside a wall and colonies in a soffit or eave.

Roof repairs here follow hurricane rules

Building code treats this county as hurricane country at its strictest: the state code places Miami-Dade in its High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, which comes with more stringent requirements. The county's Product Control section approves or denies roofing, windows, doors and other products that protect a building's envelope. So a tile reset after a tile roof hive is not just stacking tiles back in place; the underlayment, fasteners and tile have to suit that code. Whether a permit is needed depends on how much is opened and where you live, and your local building department makes that call.

Why removal and repair stay together

Splitting the job between a removal outfit and a separate contractor leaves a gap: an open wall waiting for someone to call back, and a contractor who never saw what was behind it. Keeping both under one roof means the people who cut the opening know exactly how far the comb reached and what was scraped out. You get one quote and, if you ask, an itemized invoice and photos of the cleaned cavity before it was closed. Those photos help with a home sale or inspection, and the cost page explains how repairs change the total.

How the repair side of the job runs

  1. Map the comb first

    Thermal imaging shows how far the colony spread behind the surface, so the opening is cut once to the size the comb needs instead of being widened piece by piece.

  2. Clear every scrap of comb

    Live bees are handed over for relocation first. Then comb, honey and loose wax are scraped from studs, block cells and roof deck, since residue left in the cavity ferments, stains and feeds moths.

  3. Close the old entrance

    The gap the bees used is sealed with what suits the spot, such as mortar, sealant, screen or flashing, so scouts drawn by the lingering scent find no way back in.

  4. Rebuild what was cut

    Our own licensed contractor or roofer puts the surface back: lath and stucco coats, new drywall, tile reset over fresh underlayment, or replacement soffit panels.

  5. Prime, tint and paint

    A painter primes the patch, mixes color to the weathered wall rather than the original can, and feathers the edges so the repair does not read as a square.

What we put back afterward

When the crew leaves, the cavity holds no wax or honey, the entrance is closed, and the patched area blends into the rest of the house. Cut comb, old drywall and debris go out with the crew, since clearing comb and honey happens on that same visit. The workmanship is warrantied: if bees move back into a spot we sealed, we return and handle it. Our guide to why bees return explains what draws them.

What moves the price on this job

Repairs are what push a job past the ground-level range of about $300 to $400. A colony in a meter box or a low hedge needs no rebuilding at all. One behind second-story stucco, under barrel tile or above a finished ceiling needs a cut, a rebuild and paint, and those jobs can run into the thousands. A free quote breaks removal and repair into separate lines. With the bees gone and only the opening left to close, pick a weekday slot: after-hours and weekend emergencies cost more.

Where repairs need extra care in Miami-Dade

  • Coral Gables

    Incorporated in 1925 around Mediterranean Revival design, Coral Gables has a Board of Architects whose sign-off covers work down to roof tiles, color and materials, so a stucco or tile patch must keep the original look.

  • Miami Beach

    The Architectural District in Miami Beach holds 960 historic buildings and the world's largest collection of Art Deco. On a stucco facade there, a patch has to follow the surrounding texture closely or it shows from the sidewalk.

  • Miami Springs

    Miami Springs, incorporated in 1926, took Pueblo Revival as its style, with rounded corners, parapets and stuccoed walls. A patch on a curved edge is shaped by hand, not sprayed flat.

  • Homestead

    When Andrew came ashore as a Category 5 storm on August 24, 1992, Homestead was among the places hit hardest. Any roof opened for a hive there has to be closed to High-Velocity Hurricane Zone standards, like everywhere else in Miami-Dade.

Questions people ask us

Can my own handyman do the patch after the bees are out?

Yes. If you go that route, we still strip the comb and seal the entrance before leaving, and the warranty covers the spot we sealed. The patch itself is then your contractor's work. Keeping it with us means the person rebuilding the wall already knows what was cut, how deep, and why.

Why not just fill the hole with expanding foam?

Foam over comb traps honey and wax inside the wall. The honey can seep out and stain, moth larvae feed on the wax, and the old nest scent stays behind for the next swarm to find. Foam is also no base for stucco or paint, so the patch would still have to be redone.

How close will a stucco patch get to my original wall texture and color?

Texture is matched by hand to whatever is on the wall, whether smooth troweled, sand floated or sprayed. Paint is tinted to the faded color, not the original code. On a wall that has weathered unevenly, the cleanest result can mean painting the face out to the next corner.

Do roof repairs after a bee removal need a permit?

It depends on how much of the roof is opened and where the house is, and your local building department decides. Whatever goes back on has to meet the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone rules that cover Miami-Dade.

Will a honey stain on my ceiling bleed through new paint?

It can, if the stained board stays in place. Honey and wax soak into drywall paper and keep working through paint. The reliable fix is replacing the stained section; where that is not practical, a stain-blocking primer goes on before the finish coat.

Before any exterior work, our condo board asks for insurance papers. What can you provide?

Yes. Certificates of insurance are available for condos, HOAs and businesses, and photos plus an itemized invoice can be provided on request. See our condo and high-rise page for how building access and board approval fit into the removal.
