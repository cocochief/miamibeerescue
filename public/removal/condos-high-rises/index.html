Towers, boards and shared walls

Getting Bees Out of Miami-Dade Condo Towers Alive

A colony twenty floors up is a shared-property problem as much as a bee problem. The job gets planned around the association, the manager, the elevator calendar and the neighbors on every side of the spot.

Quick answer

Call before anyone sprays, tapes over the opening or bangs on the railing. Honey bees nest in enclosed hollows, so in a condo tower they settle into planter boxes, rooftop equipment housings, garage joints and the space under pool deck pavers. The colony leaves the tower alive in a carrier bound for a beekeeper, and the gap they used gets sealed afterward. Management gets a certificate of insurance in advance, the service elevator is booked, and residents near the spot are kept clear.

If anyone is being stung

Someone under attack on a balcony or deck should go inside at once, slide the door shut behind them and step back from the glass. Do not swat or spray. Dial 911 for wheezing, a swelling tongue or lips, dizziness, or more stings than you can count. Then call us: people being stung come ahead of every other job, and our 24-hour response guarantee applies.

What residents and staff are reporting

  • Bees crowding one corner of a balcony planter box, landing and vanishing into the soil line, while the plants in it look completely untouched.
  • Bees circling a rooftop chiller or air handler enclosure, first noticed by a mechanical contractor or the maintenance crew on a routine check.
  • Bees working a crack in the parking garage ceiling or a seam between slabs, with a few landing on windshields parked underneath.
  • A dark, humming mass of bees draped over a pool deck railing, a light fixture or the trunk of a deck palm.
  • Bees showing up inside a unit near the sliding door track, a bathroom exhaust grille or an outlet on an exterior wall.

What is going on behind it

Two different things can be going on. A clump hanging in the open is a swarm in transit: the old queen and thousands of workers waiting until scouts agree on a cavity, which rarely takes more than about three days. A tight stream of bees at one fixed opening means a colony has moved in and is building comb inside a void, and a tall building is full of them: hollow planters, mechanical housings, garage ceilings, gaps behind stucco, pedestal-set pavers. Africanized honey bees have spread through Florida and can chase a perceived threat for hundreds of meters, so a colony beside a busy pool deck or stairwell calls for careful, prompt handling.

Who is responsible for the spot the bees chose

In a condominium, the first question is often ownership. Florida's Condominium Act puts upkeep of the common elements on the association, but the declaration can assign upkeep of limited common elements, the parts reserved for one unit or a few, to the owners who use them. Your building's documents decide which side of the line a balcony planter falls on. Tell management as soon as you notice bees, so the board and the owner work from a single plan. A quote can go to either party, and photos with an itemized invoice help settle who pays. Managers with several properties can see the HOA and property manager page, and owners who rent out a unit may want the tenant and landlord guide.

Working at height without dropping anything on anyone

On a high balcony, the work happens from inside the unit and the balcony itself, never from a ladder over a railing, and tarps catch anything that could fall toward the walkway below. Disturbed bees fly out in every direction, so neighbors on adjacent balconies are asked to shut their doors. Roof access runs through the building engineer, and nearby equipment may need to be paused by whoever services it. A thermal camera picks out the warm mass of comb behind a planter wall or housing panel, so only that section is opened. More on what a thermal scan shows, and on colonies under membranes and parapets on the flat and tile roof page.

Garages, pool decks and the amenity level

The podium and ground levels give bees what they want. A pool means steady water, deck palms offer shade and hollows, and garages hide pipe chases, electrical rooms and expansion joints that stay dark and dry. These are shared spaces with constant traffic, so timing counts as much as method: a deck section closed for a morning, garage work set for the quiet hours your manager names. See the pool equipment page for pump and filter rooms, the utility box page for meter and valve rooms, and the palm and tree page for colonies in deck palms.

Inspections and concrete restoration can stir colonies up

Florida's Senate Bill 4-D, passed in 2022 after the 2021 collapse in Surfside, requires milestone structural inspections for condominium and cooperative buildings three habitable stories or taller and ended the option to waive structural reserves. Miami-Dade already had a 40-year building inspection program dating to the mid-1970s. The result is more engineers sounding balconies and more restoration crews opening slabs, planters and railings, and any of them can break into a hidden colony without warning. If your building is heading into an inspection or repair project, have the bees taken out before scaffolding or swing stages go up, while the area is still easy to reach and close off.

How a building job runs, start to finish

  1. Paperwork before the visit

    Management receives a certificate of insurance naming the association and its management company, plus a site contact, a booked service elevator and a short notice for residents near the spot.

  2. Mapping where the comb sits

    A thermal camera traces the colony through the planter wall, slab edge or equipment panel, so only the section that holds comb is opened and the rest of the finish stays intact.

  3. Clearing people from the area

    Pool deck sections get taped off, neighboring balconies are asked to keep doors shut, and garage work is set for the hour your manager says traffic is lightest.

  4. Bees and comb come out

    A soft vacuum lifts the bees into an aired carrier, brood comb rides along with them to a beekeeper who keeps the colony, and honey comb is bagged so nothing drips onto the floors or cars below.

  5. Sealing and the handover

    The opening is closed, the area is wiped down, and management receives photos and an itemized invoice if they want them for the board.

What we put back afterward

A hollow that still smells of wax and honey invites the next swarm, so the comb is cut out down to the last piece before the entry point is closed. Where stucco, a planter cap, a ceiling panel or a roof edge had to be opened, the patch is the job of our own licensed trades (contractors, roofers, painters), matched to the finish and colors your association approved. Honey residue on concrete is cleaned off. That workmanship carries a warranty, and a return of bees to a closed spot brings the crew back to the building. The patching process has its own page on repairs after removal, and leftover wax and honey are covered under comb and honey cleanup.

What moves the price on this job

Where the bees are decides most of the price. A swarm on a pool deck palm or a lobby-level railing counts as ground-level work, which runs roughly $300 to $400. A colony built into a rooftop enclosure, a slab void or a high planter needs more time, more coordination and often repairs, so those jobs can reach the thousands. The estimate itself is free. If stinging residents force an overnight or weekend visit, it is billed higher than one booked inside weekday building hours. Where this comes up in Miami-Dade

    Brickell, inside the City of Miami, is the city's densest neighborhood, a district of condominium and apartment towers where colonies end up in amenity-deck planters, garage levels and rooftop equipment areas.

Questions people ask us

Does the association or the unit owner pay when bees nest in a balcony planter?

Your declaration answers that. If it assigns upkeep of your balcony to you as the owner, the cost is probably yours; if the association maintains it, the board usually pays. Let management know before booking anything. A quote can go to either side, and photos with an itemized invoice give the board what it needs to decide.

Our building only allows vendors on weekdays during set hours. Can you work with that?

Yes. Non-emergency visits are scheduled inside the hours your building permits, with the service elevator reserved ahead. If bees are stinging residents and the work has to happen at night or on a weekend, that visit costs more, and the manager approves the timing before anyone arrives.

Can the certificate of insurance name both the association and its management company?

Yes. Coverage is in place because the business is licensed and insured, and the certificate can list the association and its management company by name. Pass along the exact legal names and any wording the carrier asks for, and it goes to the office before a date is set.

If nobody touches the swarm hanging from our pool deck railing, does it fly off by itself?

A resting swarm often moves on within a few days once its scouts pick a home, but that home could be a planter or wall on your own building. Keep residents away from it and call for a swarm pickup. The guide on scout bees explains the warning signs.

Someone sprayed the bees in our planter, and now they are back. Why?

Spraying kills bees at the entrance but leaves the comb, honey and scent inside, which signal a proven nest site to the next swarm. Clearing that leftover material is what finally ends the cycle. Our guide on why bees come back goes into the details.
