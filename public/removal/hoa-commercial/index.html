If anyone is being stung

If bees are stinging someone on the property right now, move everyone indoors or into cars, shut the doors and stop anyone from swatting. If anyone's lips, tongue or throat swell, or breathing turns noisy or hard, that is a 911 call. Then phone us: the line is staffed at any hour, and stung people come first.

What residents, tenants or staff might spot

  • A steady stream of bees using one gap in a clubhouse soffit, a light pole base or the monument sign at the front gate.
  • Kitchen staff noticing bees around the dumpster enclosure, the grease bin or a vent above the back door.
  • A custodian or teacher pointing out bees at a shade structure, a portable classroom skirt or a cracked block wall near the playground.
  • Warehouse crews seeing bees at a dock door seal, a rooftop unit or a stack of pallets nobody has moved in weeks.
  • Three separate emails from different owners about the same bees, each writer assuming somebody else already made the call.

What is going on behind it

A ball of bees on a hedge that is gone by tomorrow was a swarm in transit, waiting on scouts to choose a cavity. Once that group moves into a void, such as the hollow cells of a block wall, a sign cabinet or an irrigation valve box, it starts building wax comb and filling it with brood and honey. From then on it is a settled colony, and it will not pack up because the area gets busy. On a commercial site the risk comes from routine work: a landscaper's mower, a pressure washer or a tenant jamming a broom into the hole. Nobody can tell from the sidewalk whether a colony carries Africanized genetics, and those bees defend in greater numbers and chase much farther from the nest.

Paperwork your board or owner will ask for

Approval on an association or commercial site runs up a chain: a resident reports the hive, a manager collects bids, and a board or owner releases the money. Each of those people will want documents, not only a cleared wall. Request a certificate of insurance at booking and the holder line can carry your association's name, your management firm's or the building owner's, which most vendor files insist on before a crew sets foot on common grounds.

For a homeowners' association, Florida's records statute lists itemized, detailed records of all receipts and expenditures among the official records, along with insurance policies, and says most of those records are kept for at least seven years. Bids for work are official records too, held for a year. An itemized invoice and a set of photos, both available on request, slot straight into that file. If the board wants to see numbers first, the cost page explains how a removal is priced, and the quote form gets a written figure started.

Restaurants, schools, warehouses and retail

Each kind of property puts bees somewhere different, and each has its own people to keep clear.

  • Restaurants: sugary waste and spilled syrup draw foragers, while colonies may settle in the hollow walls and roof edges around the kitchen. See bees inside block walls and soffit and eave hives.
  • Schools: the work zone has to be closed off from students, so timing and a wide cordon come before any tools come out.
  • Warehouses and flex space: dock seals, parapets and rooftop units hide colonies, which matters in a town like Medley, where industrial development makes up the main tax base. Our roof hive page covers access at height.
  • Retail centers: pylon signs, planters, parking-lot palms and buried meter and valve boxes sit right where shoppers walk.

Working around open hours and residents

Where the bees are not bothering anyone yet, booking a weekday visit with one section taped off keeps the bill lower, because night and weekend emergency calls are charged at a higher rate than weekday visits. When bees are actually stinging customers or residents, the hour stops mattering: phones are picked up 24/7 and people being stung are handled first, through our emergency removal line.

For an association, it helps to name one contact who can open gates, the pool room or the roof hatch, and to post a short notice so owners know why an area is closed. Landlords and tenants sorting out who calls can read renting with bees. For a tower rather than a low-rise community, the condo and high-rise page covers balconies, risers and board approvals.

How a managed-property removal runs

  1. Quote and paperwork first

    You send photos and the address. A free quote comes back, and if your vendor rules call for it, a certificate of insurance naming the association or owner.

  2. Access, parking and a cordon

    Gate codes, keys, roof access and a spot for the truck get settled in advance, then the work area is taped off so residents, shoppers or students stay well back.

  3. Find the comb with thermal imaging

    A living colony holds its brood at a steady warmth, and a thermal camera picks up that heat pattern behind stucco or a sign face, so the cut lands on the comb. More in what thermal imaging shows.

  4. Cut out comb, keep bees living

    Working section by section, the crew slices comb free and vacuums bees at low suction into a screened carrier, and a beekeeper takes delivery to give the colony a new home.

  5. Seal, clean and close the file

    The cavity is scraped and sealed, and on request you get photos of each stage with an itemized invoice that lists every line of work.

What we put back afterward

Honey left in a wall ferments, drips through drywall and draws ants, roaches and new swarms that smell the old nest, so every scrap of comb comes out first (comb cleanup explains why). Nobody else is subbed in for the patch. Masonry and stucco go to our own licensed contractors, tile goes to our roofers, and our painters finish the color, so a single vendor answers for every layer of a common-area wall. Should a colony try the same sealed opening again, the workmanship warranty covers a return trip to deal with it. Why bees come back covers the reasons.

What moves the price on this job

Two numbers frame most quotes. Anything reachable from the ground, such as a valve box or the base of a monument sign, is about $300 to $400. Expect the figure to rise into the thousands once comb sits behind a parapet, inside a two-story wall or under roof tile and the opening has to be rebuilt and painted. Emergency trips after dark or on weekends add to the total compared with booking a weekday slot. Quoting is free, and an itemized invoice, on request, breaks out removal and repair so your accountant can code each line.

  • Lift rental, roof hatch access or scaffold to reach a parapet or second-floor wall
  • Size of the nest: a new swarm's few combs versus seasons of stored honey
  • Repair scope: block, stucco, roof tile, trim and paint matching
  • Calendar timing, since emergency trips at night or on weekends carry a premium

Questions people ask us

Will the COI name our association as certificate holder?

It can. Every job is backed by licensed and insured coverage, and the certificate can list the association, the management company or the owner as holder. Mention it when you call or fill in the form, along with any wording your vendor file requires, so it arrives before the work date.

Our pest vendor offered to spray. Why choose live removal?

A dead colony is still a wall full of wax and stored honey. With no bees left to tend it, the wax softens in the heat and the honey ferments and leaks into drywall or stucco; ants and roaches follow, and the leftover scent tells the next swarm the hollow is ready. Taking the bees out alive means the comb leaves with them, the colony ends up with a beekeeper, and the entry gets closed.

Who should make the call: the manager, the board or the tenant?

Anyone can report the bees and get the quote moving, and a photo from a tenant is a perfectly good start. The work order just needs approval from whoever pays and controls access, so loop in the manager or a board member early to avoid a second trip.

What will the invoice show for our records?

On request, an itemized invoice lists the removal, any cut-out, the sealing and each repair line separately, and photos show the opening before, during and after. That suits an association that has to keep detailed records of its expenditures, and it gives an owner proof the work was finished.

Can the work happen at night or on a weekend so the store stays open?

Night and weekend emergency calls are billed above the weekday rate, so the cheaper path is usually a weekday visit with the affected area roped off. If bees are threatening customers or staff, call whatever the hour, because the phones are answered 24/7.
