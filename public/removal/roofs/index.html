Roof colonies, tile by tile

Bees in Your Roof in Miami: Barrel Tile, Flat Tile and Parapet Roofs

A colony under roof tile is a hive living in the gap between the tile and the felt, warmed by the sun all day. Getting it out means lifting tile without breaking the roof's seal.

Quick answer

When bees keep slipping under one particular tile, or into a crack at the edge of a flat roof, a colony has probably built comb up there. Spraying the opening only kills the bees it reaches and leaves wax and honey baking on the roof deck. The fix is to find the comb with a thermal camera, lift the tiles or roofing over it, take the bees out alive for a beekeeper, scrape out every scrap of comb, and have our own licensed roofers put the roof back.

If anyone is being stung

Someone stung? Move them inside and close the doors and windows facing the roof. A tight throat, a puffy face or labored breathing is a 911 call before anything else. After that, call us at any hour: stung people jump the queue, and the emergency crew works to our 24-hour response guarantee.

What you may be seeing on the roof

  • Foragers landing at the open mouth of a single barrel tile by the eave and vanishing inside, all day long.
  • Bees crawling over a cracked or slipped tile, or around a gap where the ridge or hip cap has lifted.
  • On a flat roof, bees working a seam in the parapet cap, a loose flashing edge or the space around a roof drain.
  • A hum you can hear through a ceiling that sits right under the roof.
  • Dark, sticky streaks running down the tile, the stucco under the eave or the inside of a ceiling.
  • A cluster hanging off the roof edge that never moves on after a day or two.

What is going on behind it

Honey bees look for a dry, dark cavity with one small way in, and a tile roof is full of them. Each barrel tile is a curved cap over a curved pan, and the hollow under it runs uphill like a tunnel. Flat tile leaves a thinner gap above the underlayment. A flat roof hides its cavity inside the parapet or between the roof deck and the ceiling below. Once a swarm moves in, the workers hang sheets of wax comb from the underside of the tile or deck and fill them with brood and honey. The brood nest is held in the low-to-mid 90s Fahrenheit, which is why it glows on a thermal camera. Comb left under sun-baked tile with no bees to cool it can soften and leak honey into the roof.

Barrel tile, flat tile and flat roofs need different handling

On an S-shaped barrel tile roof, the colony can follow the channel under one row of cap tiles, so the comb may run a few feet upslope from the entrance. The tiles over it have to come off in order, get numbered and stacked, and go back in the same courses so the lines match.

Flat concrete tile lies tight to the roof, so a colony there spreads sideways in a shallow layer and the comb is thin and wide. Breakage is the risk, which is why any tile that cracks is replaced with a matching profile rather than left in place.

Parapet roofs give a colony three hiding places: the hollow of the parapet wall itself, the space under the coping, and the gap between deck and ceiling. Sometimes the cleanest way in is from the room below, through the ceiling, so the membrane on top stays untouched. When the only way is from above, the roofing is cut, the comb comes out, and the membrane is patched and sealed. Hives that started at the roof edge often belong on our soffit and eave page instead.

Why a roofer has to close the roof in Miami-Dade

Miami-Dade sits inside the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone of the Florida Building Code, which carries stricter requirements than most of the state. A roofing product approved by the Miami-Dade Product Control Section carries a Notice of Acceptance, and a copy of that notice goes in with the building permit application. Florida statute also treats roofing as its own contracting trade, covering the install, repair and alteration of every kind of roof.

For you, that means the person who lifts your tile should not be the only one who puts it back. Our own licensed roofers reset the tile, replace broken pieces and repair felt or membrane so the roof stays a proper roof, not a patch left by a bee crew. Repairs after removal covers the wider set of trades we use.

Leaving the comb up there costs more later

Killing the bees and walking away is the version that goes wrong. With no workers left to fan it and guard it, the wax softens in the heat, honey seeps down through the underlayment and into the ceiling, and the smell draws wax moths, beetles, ants and roaches. It also tells the next swarm that the spot was a good home. That is the reason we take out every piece and clean the cavity. The field guide on repeat colonies explains the scent trail, and the comb and honey cleanup page covers the scraping.

How the colony comes off the roof

  1. Scan before lifting anything

    A thermal camera, pointed at the tile from the roof or at the ceiling from inside, shows where the warm brood nest sits so we open only the tiles over it. See what a thermal scan shows.

  2. Lift and number the tile

    Tiles over the colony come off one course at a time, are marked for position and stacked safely on the roof or the ground. Cracked pieces are set aside for matching.

  3. Move the bees out alive

    Brood comb is cut out in pieces and banded into frames while a low-suction bee vacuum collects the workers off the tile, then a beekeeper takes the whole colony. Where it ends up is covered under colony relocation.

  4. Strip and clean the cavity

    All comb and honey is scraped off the tile, battens and felt, the area is wiped down, and the residue that would call in the next swarm is gone.

  5. Seal, reset and close the entry

    Our roofers repair underlayment or membrane, reset tile in its original courses, replace broken pieces and close the gap the bees used.

What we put back afterward

On a tile roof, the putback is the tile itself: every lifted piece reset in its course, matching replacements for any that cracked, felt repaired where comb or honey damaged it, and the eave opening the bees used closed off. On a flat roof, the cut is patched and sealed and the parapet or flashing gap is shut. Inside, any stained ceiling can be repaired and painted by our own contractors and painters. Our workmanship is warrantied on roof work as well: should bees settle again behind tile we closed, a crew comes back to your roof.

What moves the price on this job

Ground-level jobs sit around $300 to $400, and a roof is rarely ground level. Roof work usually costs more, because it adds climbing, lifting and resetting tile, and the roofing repair on top. A large hive spread under many courses, with broken tile to replace and felt or a ceiling to fix, can climb into the thousands. Questions people ask us

Will you break my barrel tile getting the hive out?

Old clay and concrete tile can crack when it is lifted, and some pieces will. Each lifted tile is numbered and stacked, and any that crack are replaced with a matching profile by our own licensed roofers. You will see the replacements itemized on the invoice if you ask for it, along with before and after photos.

Is spraying the gap under a tile enough to end it?

Spray kills the bees you can reach and leaves the comb, the brood and the honey behind. In a hot roof that wax softens, honey runs into the felt and ceiling, and pests move in. It also leaves a scent that draws a new swarm. Taking the colony out alive and cleaning the cavity is what actually ends it.

Do the bees have to come out from the top of a flat roof?

Not always. When the comb sits between the deck and the ceiling, opening the ceiling from inside can be cleaner and keeps the roofing membrane intact. The thermal scan shows where the nest sits, and we choose the route that means the least repair for you.

My HOA manages the roof. Who should call you?

Whoever is responsible for the roof can make the call, owner or manager. Shared roofs often come with a board rule that a COI be on file before a crew climbs up, so we send that certificate directly to the manager, while the unit owner still receives the photos and invoice. For boards, the HOA and property manager page covers what else to request.

Bees just landed on my roof edge. Is that a hive yet?

Out in the open like that, the cluster is a swarm resting between homes, and its scouts may be checking gaps like the ones under your tile. It may leave by itself, or it may move in. Read about scout bees, and if it stays put, see swarm pickup.

A roof inspection during a sale found bees. What now?

Get the colony out before closing so the repair can be documented. Photos and an itemized invoice help both sides of the deal. Our guide on bees found during a home sale walks through timing, and a roof damaged in a storm has its own notes in colonies exposed after a storm.
